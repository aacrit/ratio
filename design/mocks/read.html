The reading

  1. Proportionon the mark0.64 : 0.36

    Measured The break sits at 0.64 of the height, head to feet.

    Rule A break near a third (0.333) or the golden section (0.382) of the height reads as composed; near-equal halves read as boxy.

    Advice The break sits near the golden section from below (0.618): a long upper piece over a short lower one. It is the second classical division. Keep the lower block narrow.

  2. Volumeon the mark1.45× · 0.55×

    Measured Upper piece 1.45× the shoulder width at mid-torso; both legs 0.55× at the knee.

    Rule One fitted volume against one fuller one keeps a line; two full volumes read as unanchored, two fitted ones as streamlined.

    Advice Loose over narrow: one full volume balanced by one fitted, the classic pairing. Keep it.

  3. Leg lineon the markΔL 0.06

    Measured Lightness of the lower piece against the shoes.

    Rule Shoes close in value to the lower piece continue the leg line; shoes in strong contrast end it at the ankle.

    Advice The shoes sit close in value to the lower piece (0.30 and 0.36), so the leg line runs unbroken to the floor.

  4. Harmonyon the markY · 105° 255° 25°

    Measured 6 colours in the outfit: cream (105°), black, cream (105°), charcoal, navy (255°), oxblood (25°).

    Rule Matsuda's hue templates (monochromatic, complementary, analogous, right-angle, analogous with a complementary accent, double complementary, half wheel), fitted on the perceptual OKLCH wheel. A palette inside one reads as harmonious.

    Advice The hues fit the analogous-with-a-complementary-accent template: neighbouring hues (cream, oxblood) with one hue from across the wheel (navy), a classic harmony. Keep any new piece inside it, or neutral.

  5. Valuefinerange 0.74

    Measured Lightness of each colour: 0.93, 0.19, 0.78, 0.40, 0.30, 0.36.

    Rule Lightness carries form before hue does. A darker value below a lighter one grounds a figure; the widest lightness edge draws the eye first.

    Advice Light over dark (0.93 above 0.30): the weight sits low and the figure reads grounded. Overall, a strong value range: the edges between light and dark are what the eye sees first.

  6. Colour sharesfine0.25 · 0.25 · 0.20

    Measured Share of the outfit's area: 0.25, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.05.

    Rule Colour by area: one dominant, one secondary, one accent, near 0.60 · 0.30 · 0.10. Equal shares compete for the lead.

    Advice A dominant colour at 0.25 with the rest supporting it. The 60-30-10 proportion (0.60 · 0.30 · 0.10) is the reference: a smaller third colour would sharpen it into an accent.

  7. Chromaon the mark, borderline1 saturated

    Was fine, now on the mark: the oxblood shoes give the outfit its one saturated note.

    Measured Chroma of each colour: 0.03, 0.00, 0.02, 0.01, 0.07, 0.11.

    Rule One saturated colour among muted ones reads as a single voice. Complements at equal lightness vibrate where they meet (Albers).

    Advice One saturated colour among quieter ones: a single voice, the way a painter places the brightest note. The palette is warm and cool in balance.
