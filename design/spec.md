# Ratio: design spec (G2)

Studio, 2026-10-04, on top of the premium redesign (`spike/suggest-try`); revised after the user-sim dry run (Sam, Mara) the same day. Law: `design/BRAND.md` (Cloth and Chalk) under Ink & Momentum. Mocks: `design/mocks/{read,face,rules,card}.html` on the shared frame `design/mocks/frame.css`; CI shoots each at 375, 768 and 1280 (`scripts/mock-shots.mjs`). Copy here is final; a builder copies it verbatim. No exclamation marks, no em dashes, no judgement of the person.

Breakpoints: phone (to 767), tablet (768 to 1023), desktop (1024 up, the stage 61.8% beside the sheet 38.2%). Everything is dark-first with the Paper theme following the device; the Style Card is always Paper.

## The bar and its tabs (every surface)

**Job.** Say where you are and let you move, in 52 px, without taking the stage.

**Layout.** Left: the mark (plumb drop on load) and "Ratio" (the eyebrow "reason and proportion" from 768; the name hidden under 420). Centre-left: four tabs, Inter 500 0.92 rem, 44 px minimum touch width, in a scrollable strip if the bar is too narrow: **Read · Face · Rules · Card**. Right: at most one action, always present on Read (`Read my outfit` before a read, `Read another` once one exists; nothing on Face, Rules and Card). It opens the read menu (CoS call, 2026-10-04: losing the sample and the camera after the first read was a regression), a small popover anchored under it, hairline border, no shadow (`settle` preset), 44 px minimum touch height per item: `Choose a photo`, `Use the camera` (touch only, as the drop cloth's own camera button is), `Try the sample`. Esc or a click outside closes it and returns focus to the action; arrow keys move within it; it opens focused on `Choose a photo`, so `O` then Enter still means that. It is `position: fixed`, so it never shifts the page's own layout.

**Motion.** One chalk underline, 2 px, slides and resizes to the chosen tab with `underline` (420 / 41 / 1, critically damped, 420 ms). The current tab is chalk, the others chalk-muted; hover is a colour transition over `--dur-fast`. Reduced motion: the underline jumps.

**Copy.** Tabs: `Read` `Face` `Rules` `Card`. Action: `Read another`. `aria-current="page"` on the current tab; the nav is labelled `Surfaces`.

**Surfaces that are not yet built** (Face before its ticket): the tab opens the page's drop cloth with the line `Face arrives after the Rulebook. Read an outfit meanwhile.` and the action `Read an outfit`. Never a dead tab.

## Read `/` (as built, plus the wardrobe)

**Job.** Read one full-length photo in the tab and show the measurement, the rule and the advice; let you try a look and keep the read.

**Layout** as built (BRAND, Stage and sheet). Phone: the stage holds the photo above a sheet with three resting places (peek: the verdict, large, then the ratio numeral and its eyebrow beneath it, smaller; half: the save row, the palette, the first look; full: the argument). Tablet: the same, with 24 px gutters in the sheet. Desktop: stage left, side panel right.

**Hierarchy (founder, 2026-10-04, T3): the verdict leads.** `Works.` or `Two changes would help. ...` sits large, in the display serif, as the sheet head's headline. The ratio numeral sits directly under it, smaller but still tabular and in the tape colour, with its rule named beside it in the mono eyebrow (`PROPORTION, ON THE MARK`). The photo's own tape label (the floating numeral drawn over the break line) is unchanged by this: it is a separate, smaller reading, not the hero. The count animation, on the first read of a session, lands the ratio numeral in this new place; later reads settle at once (T3, R-09).

**States and copy.**

Ratio reads the outfit: proportion and colour.` Primary button `Read my outfit` (opens the camera on a touch screen, the file picker elsewhere; the tip on touch: `For a full-length photo, ask someone to stand three steps back with the phone at waist height, your head to your feet in the frame.`), then `Try a sample: David's Napoleon` (ghost). `The sample is a painting: Jacques-Louis David, Napoleon in his study, 1812.`

## Face `/face`

**Job.** Read one head-and-shoulders portrait: the face's proportions as the input, hair choices as the advice, one portrait note. Never a verdict on the face, never a score.

**Hero.** The portrait on the stage with the face oval (dashed chalk construction line), the tape from hairline to chin with the canon's thirds (`1/3` `2/3` in chalk, 11 px mono, never the fraction glyphs, which read as `%` when small), the four measured divisions across the face in tape (hairline, brow, nose base, chin) with each third's share as a numeral to the right, the width line at the cheekbones, and the length-to-width ratio as the hero numeral by the tape. Layout, sheet and breakpoints as Read.

**Input.** A separate portrait photo (founder: a face in a full-length photo is too small to measure honestly). Face landmarker (478 points, 52 blendshapes) and the multiclass segmenter's hair mask, on the CPU delegate; no skin pixel is read until R2 passes.

**States and copy.**
- Idle: `Drop a head-and-shoulders photo. It stays in this tab.` / `One adult, facing the camera, hair and shoulders in the frame. Ratio reads the hair against the face's proportions, and adds a portrait note. Never a verdict on the face.` Buttons `Read a portrait` `Try the sample` `Take a photo` (tip: `Hold the phone at eye level, an arm's length away, with the hair and the shoulders in the frame.`). Primary `Read my portrait`, then `Try a sample: Dürer's self-portrait`. `The sample is a painting: Albrecht Dürer, Self-portrait at 28, 1500.`
- Loading and measuring as Read (`Loading the measuring models, 3.1 of 3.9 MB.` the face model is small; `Measuring.`).
- Errors: `No face found facing the camera. Use a portrait with the whole face in the frame, head on.` / `More than one face in the frame. Ratio reads one portrait at a time.` / browser and model errors as Read.
- Read (the mock): the verdict leads: `Works. The hair sits where the face's proportions want it: width at the sides, a centre parting, length past the shoulders.`; eyebrow `Sample: Dürer's self-portrait · 1.62× length to width, on the mark`; the line `These read the hair against the face's proportions. Never the face itself, and never a score.`; the save row as Read. Rows, each Measured / Rule / Advice:
  1. **Volume** `1.62× · sides 2.40×` on the mark. Measured `Face length 1.62× its width (hairline to chin, over the widest line at the cheekbones). Hair 2.40× the face width at the cheekbones, 0.22 of the face length above the hairline.` Rule `Volume answers proportion: width at the sides shortens the read of a long oval, height at the crown lengthens a short one. Between 1.40 and 1.60 either works.` Advice `Width at the sides is what a 1.62× oval asks for, and the hair as worn carries it there. Keep the volume at the sides; a cut that stacks height at the crown would stretch the read.` Edges: below 1.40 height at the crown; 1.40 to 1.60 either; 1.60 and above width at the sides; the hair gives width at 1.30× the face width or more, height at 0.15 of the face length or more. Bins 0.02 and 0.05.
  2. **Thirds** `0.26 · 0.38 · 0.36` fine. Measured `Hairline to brow 0.26, brow to nose base 0.38, nose base to chin 0.36 of the face length.` Rule `The classical canon divides the face into three parts, hairline to brow, brow to nose base, nose base to chin, and treats equal thirds as its reference. Hair sets where the first line reads.` Advice `The upper third reads shortest of the three. Swept back or parted, as worn, the hair shows it at full length; a fringe lowers the visible hairline, so that third reads shorter. Either is a choice; this is what each does to the read.` (When the upper third reads longest: `The upper third reads longest. A fringe at the brow, or a parting with volume forward, moves the visible line down and evens the thirds; swept back keeps it as it is.`)
  3. **Parting** `centre · 0.50` fine. Measured `The parting meets the hairline at 0.50 of the face width.` Rule `A centre parting mirrors the two halves of the face and reads as still; a parting at the third (0.33 or 0.67) sets the hair asymmetrically and reads as movement.` Advice `Centred, with length on both sides, the parting makes the symmetry this portrait is built on. Keep it for a formal portrait; move it to 0.33 for a less formal one.` No parting found: `No parting read: the hair meets the hairline as one line.`
  4. **Length** `0.62 below the chin` fine. Measured `The hair ends 0.62 face lengths below the chin, past the shoulder line.` Rule `Where the hair ends is a horizontal line the eye reads with the chin and the shoulders: at the jaw it widens, at the collarbone it lengthens, below the shoulder it frames.` Advice `Ending below the shoulders, the hair frames the face and the collar instead of cutting across either. Keep the ends below the shoulder line or above the jaw; between the two, the line competes with the chin.` Edges: at the jaw to 0.05; to the collarbone 0.05 to 0.40; below the shoulder 0.40 and above.
  5. **Expression** `smile 0.04 · eyes 0.62` a portrait note. Measured `Mouth smile 0.04, eyes open 0.62, brow raise 0.00, each on a 0 to 1 scale. Level gaze at the camera.` Rule `A note for the portrait, never about the person: what the expression does for the picture. A smile that reaches the eyes reads as warm; a closed mouth and a level gaze read as formal. Both are portraits.` Advice `A level gaze and a closed mouth: the formal portrait. Keep it for that. For a warm headshot, a smile that reaches the eyes, with both scores up.` Only for portraits; never on Read.
  6. **Personal colour** `after the accuracy check`, state `not yet`, muted: `Hair-to-skin contrast and undertone, as input to a palette and never as a label. It arrives after the skin-tone accuracy check (R2): until the fixture set scores evenly across all ten Monk bands, Ratio does not read skin.` When it arrives: a stacked palette bar and `Contrast ΔL 0.48 · undertone warm` as palette input only; never a season or a name for the person.
- Credit (sample): `Albrecht Dürer, Self-portrait at Twenty-eight (Self-portrait in a fur-trimmed robe), 1500. Alte Pinakothek, Munich. Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.` Hash line `Same photo, same reading. 5b1d · ratio-engine/0.4.0 · face/0.1`. Actions `Save to wardrobe` `Download this read (free)` (a portrait read counts for the Style Card's hair line, not as an outfit).
- Sources for the drill rows on Rules: Vitruvius, De architectura III.1; Dürer, Vier Bücher von menschlicher Proportion (1528); MediaPipe Face Landmarker blendshapes. Edges uncalibrated until R1's portrait fixtures exist: the eyebrow says so.

**Motion.** `arrive`, `breathe`; then `plumb` from the crown of the oval, `chalk` draws the tape, then the four divisions top to bottom, then the width line; `lands` for the hero numeral and the thirds; `argument` for the rows. `face_reading_completed` fires when the rows show.

**Not done well within the constraints, said plainly.** Makeup as a surface of its own (the brief's "makeup palette") depends on skin reading and waits with personal colour; the mock shows only the waiting row.

## Rulebook `/rules`

**Job.** Show every rule's method: its statement, a live instrument, its edges, its maths and its sources, with your last read marked (V1).

**Opens in-app (T3, founder: the photo is never stored, not even in this browser).** The tab bar's Rules tab pushes `/rules` and swaps views; nothing unloads, so Read -> Rules -> Read restores with no re-measure. `/rules.html` still loads standalone for a direct visit or a reload. A reload has nothing in memory: Read falls back to the hand-off's bins and lines, shown on the chalk figure with "Read the photo again to see it on the photo."; looks can still be tried, on the chalk figure only.

**Layout.** A scrolling page under the bar, one column at `min(100%, 960px)`: head (eyebrow `The rulebook · ratio-engine/0.4.0`, title `Seven rules, each with its maths, its edges and its source.`, a plain-words line under the title (`In plain words: each card below is one rule, shown as something you can hold. …`), lede `Every reading is held against these and nothing else. Drag an instrument to see where a band ends. Your last read in this tab is marked on each one.` and the provenance line `The canons cited here (Euclid's section, the golden ratio, Matsuda's templates) are European and Japanese traditions, one lineage among many; the rulebook names its sources so you can weigh them.`, the chip `Your last read: the sample with navy trousers and oxblood shoes · 89f0 · today` with `Clear`; the chip names the reading last shown on Read, so the two pages always agree; beside it, `Back to your reading` (T3), hidden until a read exists, `href="/#r=<hash4>"`). Then seven cards in reading order. Phone: instrument above the words. Tablet and desktop: instrument left (256 px; 320 px for the proportion hero), words right. Each card: eyebrow (rule name · `calibrated` or `set by hand, not yet calibrated` in red chalk), the rule in Fraunces, the `Yours` line (measurement in tape, verdigris on the mark, red chalk borderline, with the state word), and a drill `The maths, the edges, the sources` (closed; opening it counts `rule_opened`). The proportion drill is open in the mock to show the anatomy.

**Instruments** (BRAND, Instrument anatomy): a handle follows the hand one to one, the numeral updates as it moves (no count: it is your hand), band names and edges sit beside the scale, your value is a fixed verdigris tick labelled `yours …`. 1. Proportion: the chalk figure with the tape and the five bands (band names beside the tape; the edge values live in the drill, so nothing collides with the `yours` tick); drag the break line (live in the mock; the text under `Yours` reads `Drag the line: 0.46, halves.`). 2. Volume: two scales (upper piece × across the shoulders with fitted / straight / loose at 1.15 and 1.40; each leg at its knee with narrow / straight / wide at 0.45 and 0.60). 3. Leg line: lower piece and shoes on one lightness scale, the ΔL bracket, the 0.12 edge drawn as a length; `yours: no shoes measured` when they were not. 4. Harmony: the OKLCH hue ring (muted, chroma 0.09), the fitted template's sectors labelled in plain words (`neighbouring hues`, `one accent across`), each hue as a dot in its own colour; a dot outside a sector is ringed in red chalk with how far (`105°, 11° outside`). Template names always carry their plain meaning: monochromatic (one hue), complementary (two hues across the wheel), analogous (neighbouring hues), right-angle (a hue and one 90° away), analogous with a complementary accent (neighbouring hues plus one hue across the wheel; Matsuda's Y, never called `split`), double complementary, half wheel (hues within one 180° arc, anywhere on the wheel). The engine's rulebook text must use the same names. 5. Value: each colour's lightness on one scale, the range bracket, the low / medium / high edges drawn as lengths. 6. Shares: the 60-30-10 reference bar with two draggable dividers above your bar in your colours. 7. Chroma: each colour on the chroma scale with the neutral (0.02) and saturated (0.11) edges, and a worked vibration pair.

**States.** No read in this tab: the chip reads `Read a photo and your values appear on each instrument.` with the action `Read a photo`; every `Yours` line reads `not read yet` and the instruments start at their band's middle. With a read: as the mock. A rule not measured (no shoes): `not read as worn: the shoes were not found in the frame.` Uncalibrated rules carry the drill note `The edges are set by hand, to be calibrated against labelled photos (docs/RISKS.md R1). Read marks this rule's advice "first estimate" until then, and this line changes to "calibrated" when it is.` (engine 0.7.0: every rule's edges are set by hand today, and Read labels their advice `Advice, first estimate`). Your last read is marked as worn (verdigris, `yours`); a look tried on Read is a second, dashed chalk marker (`look`). Engine change: the chip adds `read with ratio-engine/0.3.0; the rulebook is now 0.4.0`.

**Motion.** Cards `arrive` 80 ms apart on first paint (at most 7). Instruments: hand-held, `settle` on the handle; crossing an edge flashes the band name's glow for `--dur-fast`. Reduced motion: no stagger, no flash.

## Style Card `/card`

**Job.** Show what the wardrobe's outfits have in common, as one printed page, and sell it honestly: $12 once, with the free path beside it. It counts outfits (one per photo); a look tried on the same photo belongs to that outfit.

**Hero.** The card itself on the stage, 1 : 1.618 portrait, always Paper (`--card-*` tokens), `width: min(100%, 420px)` (460 on desktop), fitted to the room. Sheet (phone: below; desktop: the panel): hero numeral = outfits saved, the wardrobe list, the offer.

**Until the Style Card ships (T3, Forge 4 builds the wardrobe above): the Card tab previews a single read.** With a read on screen, the tab opens not to "not built yet" but to `This read, as a card.`, the one outfit's card drawn the same way `Download this read (free)` does, with its own download button. No wardrobe, no count, no offer: that is the Style Card ticket's job.

**The card's content** (built in the tab from 3 to 5 saved outfits; a portrait read contributes only a hair line): masthead `Ratio` / `STYLE CARD` and `3 outfits · 2026-10-04`; **Your break** with the outfits' chalk figures and their breaks, `Your outfits break near the golden section, counted from the floor (0.618), in 3 of 3: a long upper piece over a short lower one.`; **Volume** `Loose over narrow in 2 of 3, straight over narrow in 1. One full piece over a narrow leg is the pairing that holds.`; **Colours that worked in your looks** (swatches, plain names) `The trousers and accents the rules preferred when you tried looks: a cool lower piece under a warm neutral, with one saturated accent.`; **Your outfits' contrast** `High in all 3. Each outfit: its chalk figure, a plain title the engine composes from the pieces (`Camel coat over black`, `Grey jacket over navy`; the sample is `Sample: David's Napoleon`), `0.64 : 0.36 · 12eb · 2026-10-04`, `1 look tried: navy trousers and oxblood shoes` when one was saved, its palette, `Delete` (deletes at once; the line under the list says `Deleted. Up to 5; the oldest is replaced after that. Read another outfit and save it.` Offer: `One page of what repeats across your outfits, on paper: where they break, their volumes, the colours that worked in your looks, their contrast, the colours you wore. ## Anti-slop checklist, per surface

| Surface | 1 template? | 2 accents earned | 3 numerals mono tabular | 4 copy | 5 motion means state | 6 space |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bar and tabs | no: four words and a chalk line | chalk only | n/a | verbs and nouns, no cliché | underline = where you are | 52 px, one action |
| Read | no: stage and sheet, the photo the only colour | tape, verdigris, red chalk by rule | yes, `data-ratio` pairs | audited: no exclamation, no dash; plain colour names, degrees in Measured only | each preset names a state | peek shows the verdict |
| Face | no: the oval and thirds are the subject's own geometry | same three | yes | audited; never the face judged | plumb, chalk, lands, argument | one hero numeral, six rows on demand |
