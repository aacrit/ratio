## 2026-10-03: G1 approved; the name is Ratio
The founder approved the brief in chat (AskUserQuestion) under the working title Plumb, with the note "Approved but also plan to address the 3 risks". The founder then chose the name Ratio (in Latin, both reason and proportion): `ratio.voidvision.org`. The risk plan is `docs/RISKS.md`.

## 2026-10-03: the vision model is on-device MediaPipe, on the processor
The Gemini free tier fails V3 (free-tier content may be human-reviewed and used for training), and Workers AI vision models are neither deterministic nor large enough on the free allocation. MediaPipe's pose landmarker and multiclass selfie segmenter (Apache 2.0) run in the tab on the CPU delegate. They measure; a versioned rulebook advises. No model writes advice.

## 2026-10-03: design language under Fable, decided in chat
At the founder's request the design language was made by the Studio designer on Fable. The founder's decisions: an atelier-and-geometer world; dark first with a designed light theme; a plumb-line drop (a damped pendulum) as the signature motion; exactly three earned accents (measured line, near the golden section, borderline).

## 2026-10-03: colour theory is half the engine
The founder asked that colour combination count as much as proportion. The engine carries four colour rules over the outfit's area-weighted palette: Matsuda's harmony templates (adapted to the OKLCH wheel), value structure, 60-30-10 shares and chroma with Albers' vibration. Personal colour (hair and skin contrast, undertone) was approved as palette input only, never a label for the person, and waits for the Monk-scale fixtures and their per-band accuracy gate (risk R2).

## 2026-10-03: suggest looks and try them, on device, without an image model
The founder asked for outfit suggestions with a way to try them, without image models. The engine suggests by applying a fixed set of moves (tuck, belt, a colour swap for one piece, a darker lower piece, a muted voice, an accent) to the measured bins and re-reading each candidate with the whole rulebook; it ranks by improvement and drops any look that fixes one rule by breaking another. Trying a look recolours the photo's garment pixels in OKLab (each pixel keeps its own offset from its swatch, so folds and texture stay real); a tuck or a belt is shown on a chalk figure and by the break mark, never painted onto the photo. Built as a Blueprint spike on a branch (founder decision): nothing releases until G2, and the build slot stays with Dial. `look_tried` is counted; the per-event day ceiling moves from 4,000 to 3,000 to keep the worst-case D1 writes at 12.6% of the account.

## 2026-10-04: engine 0.6.0, read trust (T2)
From Noor's pass on the real build. The read uses only the person the pose found: the connected component of the segmenter's mask holding their shoulders, hips and knees, clipped to a corridor round the skeleton when they touch someone; one plain line says when other people are in the photo and where the read person stands, by thirds of the frame, and another when the pose could not single the person out (a known limit in docs/RISKS.md). A look's colour change is shown on the photo only when at least 60% of the cloth it would paint is the read person's, it moves at least 25% of the piece and 1% of their garment area; otherwise the chalk figure alone shows it, and its card says the photo is as worn. Shares are apportioned by largest remainder (they sum to 1.00, each within 0.05 of its measure); harmony, shares and chroma read colours that share a plain name as one, as every list on screen does, while value keeps each colour's own lightness; advice quotes only numbers its Measured line or its rule's edges show; the three looks hold at most one hue family (red, earth, green, blue, violet, neutral) per piece, and a tuck or belt takes at most two of the three slots while a look without it also helps.

## 2026-10-04: engine 0.7.0, the reading matches the photo (T4)
From UX pass 2 (Mara on two CC photos, Noor on the build). **Chroma:** a garment's colour is its well-lit core: the median lightness of its pixels between the 50th and 95th lightness quantiles, the hue of that core's summed a/b, and the median chroma projected onto that hue, so shadow, highlight and a neighbouring cloth's hue cannot average a colour to grey. The photo's neutral cast (median a/b of the person's garment pixels under chroma 0.04, when they are at least a fifth of them, capped at 0.03) is taken off every garment colour, so a uniform tint cannot make a hue; a swatch in the hue of a strongly coloured backdrop (chroma 0.08 or more) that is neither piece's colour is that light, not cloth. The palette clusters the upper and lower regions apart in OKLab with a and b weighted 2.5, joins a dark cluster carrying a colour's tint to that colour (cloth in shade), joins neutral clusters within 0.12 lightness (one cloth lit and in shade), and ignores pixels within 8% of the body's width of the silhouette. Neutral is decided on the binned colour, at or below the line. **Break:** the hem of the outer upper piece: the change point of the row colours read on the figure's flanks (never the middle, where a zip, an open front or a t-shirt shows), searched from halfway between shoulders and hips to the knee. A middle column contrasting with the flanks on 60% of the upper piece's rows marks a front opening: no tuck or belt is offered, the line suggests a shorter upper piece, and elsewhere a tuck is a condition ("if the upper piece tucks"). Two near-blacks (a black hoodie over black jeans) read as one column; their hem is a shadow, not a colour edge. **Volume:** the upper piece is read only on rows no arm or hand crosses (pose elbows and wrists), or not read and said so; each leg is read on its own run at its knee (edges 0.45 and 0.60 a leg, set by hand), and one function makes the words from the numbers. **Shoes:** read from boxes round each foot (ankle, heel, toe), the one detection the leg line, the palette and try-on share; the shoe colour is the cluster holding the box's upper half; cut off by the frame or merged with the floor is said. **Looks:** never the colour a piece wears, never the accent (kept and said), no accent on unread shoes, no look without a rule chip. **Rulebook:** every edge is set by hand, so every entry is uncalibrated and Read says "first estimate"; Euclid is struck from 60-30-10, "all warm or all cool" from the half wheel, Dürer from the provenance line. The Rulebook marks the outfit as worn and a tried look as a dashed second marker; the arrows move a handle the way they point. The screens job reads both UX-pass photos (pinned by SHA-256, never in git).

## 2026-10-04: engine 0.7.0 after review (T4, round 2)
The reviewer's probes changed four 0.7.0 methods before release. **Break:** no floor; each candidate break is scored against the median of the rows above it, on each flank of the body column separately, in a distance where hue and chroma count 2.5 times and lightness half (shading changes lightness, a hem changes the cloth); a hem must be a local edge on both flanks (the rows within 3% of the figure's height above and below differ), so a crop top's hem at 0.23 is read, while light falling off a jacket's shoulders or a t-shirt showing on one side of an open hoodie is not a hem. **Front opening:** only rows whose flanks are one even cloth count, and the opening is a narrow line (at most 8% of the width) whose colour is absent from the flanks, followed down the figure through 60% of the upper piece's height; stripes, checks, tartan and prints never read as an opening. **Cast:** accepted only when the upper and lower garments' near-neutral pools (chroma under 0.03) agree within 0.01; removed from a pixel only when that lowers its chroma and never from a pixel at or under the neutral line, so it cannot give black shoes a hue; try-on compares pixels with the cast removed. **Volume:** an arm spoils only the edge on its side (from 2 arm half-widths outside the edge to the centre line); with one clean edge the width is twice its distance from the centre line and the reading is borderline; the upper piece and the legs are said on their own when only one is read, and balance advice needs both. `tuckable(bins)` is the one predicate for every tuck. Coral starts at L 0.62 only below chroma 0.18, so scarlet stays red.

## 2026-10-04: the photo is never stored, not even in this browser (T3 review)
Founder, 2026-10-04: the photo is never stored, not even in this browser; reads are kept in memory and Rules opens in-app. The T3 session store's IndexedDB layer (pixels, masks, fullMask, kept across reloads) is removed entirely: law 1 ("the photo never leaves the tab") is read as "never leaves memory" too. Session reads live in `web/src/session.ts`'s in-memory array only, for as long as the page itself is alive. That window had to grow for Read -> Rules -> Read to still restore with no re-measure, so the Rulebook now renders in-app (`rules/render.ts`'s `renderRulebook()` into a container, `rules.ts`'s `setupRulebook()` wiring it) instead of navigating: the tab bar's Rules tab pushes `/rules` and swaps views, nothing unloads, and the in-memory session survives. `/rules.html` still loads standalone for a direct visit or a reload, built the same way. A genuine reload (or a direct visit with no in-app history) has nothing in memory: it restores from the existing lastRead hand-off instead (`rules/handoff.ts`'s sessionStorage summary, bins and lines only, never pixels), shown as the chalk figure in the outfit's measured colours with the line "Read the photo again to see it on the photo."; looks can still be tried, on the chalk figure only. `web/privacy.html` drops the IndexedDB paragraph; every remaining claim is paired with code in `tests/privacy-page.test.ts`, including a test that no code path calls `indexedDB`.

## 2026-10-04: cross-engine determinism enforced (T5); the `determinism` job is a real gate prerequisite, not advisory
R3's golden-hash enforcer (`scripts/fixture-hashes.mjs`, `fixtures.lock.json`) launches its own Chromium, Firefox and WebKit and reads the first-visit sample plus p1 and p2 through the real UI path, comparing the reading hash and bins from `web/src/rules/handoff.ts`'s sessionStorage handoff (the same thing the Rulebook page already trusts). This repo has no GitHub branch-protection required check configured for `main` (checked directly: `gh api repos/aacrit/ratio/branches/main/protection` answers 404, and `screens.yml`'s own comment says it is "advisory evidence for review, not the merge gate"). So rather than add a second workflow file whose result nothing reads, the `determinism` job lives inside `gate.yml` itself, and the `gate` job declares `needs: [determinism]`: a red or skipped `determinism` means `gate` cannot run, so it can never go green, and the `/ship` skill's existing "wait for the gate check" already holds without any change to branch protection or the skill. A fast unit test (`tests/fixtures-lock.test.ts`) catches an `ENGINE_VERSION` bump with a stale lock before the three-browser job even runs.

## 2026-10-04: engine 0.8.0, we apply a photo's colour profile, not the browser (T5)
Once Firefox actually read, p1 (an Adobe-RGB-tagged JPEG) read differently from Chromium in 20 bins. The three engines decode a tagged photo to three different buffers: Chromium converts with skcms, Firefox hands the canvas the raw values, and WebKit converts with its own arithmetic and ignores `colorSpaceConversion: "none"`. Untagged photos decode identically in all three. Every iPhone photo is tagged (Display P3), so this was a real law-2 break. `web/src/engine/icc.ts` now cuts the profile out of JPEG, PNG and WebP files before the browser decodes them, and converts matrix/curve RGB and grey profiles to sRGB itself on integer tables (pow only builds the tables, each entry rounded to 16 or 8 bits). An sRGB profile is left alone; a profile it cannot apply (LUT-only, CMYK) is dropped, and those pixels are read as sRGB, the same in every engine. **Runners:** MediaPipe tasks-vision 1.0.1 uploads every input image as a WebGL2 texture even on the CPU delegate, so the read worker needs WebGL2 on an OffscreenCanvas. The Linux CI image gives Firefox no WebGL because it has no GPU. The script overrides that blocklist, which changes no arithmetic. A WebKit worker on Linux gets no WebGL2 context at all, so WebKit is read on a macOS runner (free on a public repo), with Chromium read there too, so x86 and ARM are held to the same hashes. The lock now enforces every browser it lists as `ok`. A browser that stops reading fails the job unless the lock records it as `unsupported: <reason>`.

## 2026-10-05: engine 0.9.0, every photo is decoded by our own code (founder decision, T5)
On the macOS runner WebKit ran the models but disagreed with Chromium on all three fixtures, untagged ones included. Its own decode of the same JPEG gave different pixels (Apple ImageIO; Chromium and Firefox use libjpeg-turbo), so Safari users would get different advice for the same photo. Two options went to the founder: decode ourselves, or record WebKit as a known limit. **Decision (founder, 2026-10-05): decode ourselves; law 2 holds in Safari too.** `web/src/decode.ts` decodes JPEG with MozJPEG (`@jsquash/jpeg` 1.6.0), PNG with the Rust png crate (`@jsquash/png` 3.1.1) and WebP with libwebp (`@jsquash/webp` 1.5.0). All three are WebAssembly builds from exact-pinned npm packages (package-lock.json), bundled and served from this origin, and compiled under the existing `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` with no other CSP change. Each loads the first time its format is read, in the read worker. Any other format is refused as "not an image". `web/src/engine/orient.ts` applies EXIF orientation (JPEG APP1, PNG eXIf, WebP EXIF); the decoders never rotate. **Why MozJPEG over jpeg-js (pure JS):** it is integer throughout (ISLOW IDCT, fancy upsampling and colour conversion on integer tables). Its output on the three fixtures is bit-identical to Chromium's and Firefox's libjpeg-turbo decode, so those readings did not move, only the engine version did. In Chromium, Firefox and WebKit it gave identical pixels on all three fixtures and on a 12 MP phone-size JPEG. It decodes 12 MP in about 90 to 130 ms against jpeg-js's 700 ms, and it is Apache-2.0 and maintained (2025 release). jpeg-js's chroma upsampling differs from libjpeg's (other pixels on every fixture), and its last release was in 2022. **Cost:** the JPEG path adds 18.8 kB of glue and 166 kB of WASM (64.6 kB gzipped), loaded on the first read only; PNG and WebP add their own only when such a file is read. The read worker's own chunk is unchanged at about 206 kB. This repo has no bundle-size budget (`lint-size` limits tracked files to 5 MB), so none needed changing.

## 2026-10-05: T5 review 1, no engine's own pow, and a refused decode said as what it is
The profile conversion no longer calls `Math.pow`: a profile's curves are evaluated with `web/src/engine/detmath.ts` (log and exp from + - * / only, which IEEE-754 fixes to the bit in every engine), and the sRGB encode table is a pinned constant (`SRGB_STEPS`, hash-tested and rebuilt from the formula in `tests/colour-profile.test.ts`). On every gamma and sRGB curve tested, the 16-bit tables are identical to the `Math.pow` ones and the pinned Adobe RGB conversion is unchanged, so no measurement moved and `ENGINE_VERSION` stays 0.9.0. The per-pixel colour maths after the profile (sRGB to linear, OKLab's cube root, hue and chroma in `web/src/engine/color.ts`) still uses the engine's `**`, `Math.cbrt`, `Math.atan2` and `Math.hypot`; that remaining exposure is recorded under R3 in `docs/RISKS.md`. The profile is now only read out of the file (`extractColourProfile`); the stripped copy nothing used is gone, as are the second copies of the file and of the decoded pixels. A JPEG or WebP its decoder refuses gets its own copy (a four-component CMYK JPEG, an animated WebP, or a file that may be damaged), never "not a photo". `scripts/fixture-hashes.mjs` reads each fixture in a fresh browser context (T3 restores the last reading on load) and waits for that upload's own reading; two fixtures with one hash fail the check and `--update`, and `--update` records a browser the lock enforces as unsupported only when `--allow-unsupported <browser>` names it.

## 2026-10-07: engine 0.11.0, a tailor's words (T9); Volume's reference is "across the shoulders" (founder decision)
From UX pass 4 (R-09). **Founder decision (2026-10-07, chat):** Volume's reference is called "across the shoulders", the garment spec-sheet term, because to a tailor "shoulder line" is the seam from the neck to the shoulder point, not a width; the Rulebook still says plainly that it is the span between the two shoulder points the pose model marks. That span comes from the pose, not the cloth, so a half-read Volume ("Upper piece not read; lower piece 0.40× across the shoulders at the knee line") is true, and every ratio names it. "Shoulder line" is now in `BODY_MEASURE_WORDS`; only "across the shoulders" is allowed. **One lightness per piece:** the value line read the measured core (0.26 on p1) while the leg line read the swatch (0.32); every line now reads the piece's swatch (`pieceLightness` in `web/src/engine/rules.ts`), the number the palette strip, the Measured list and the chalk figure already show, and the core only picks which swatch is which piece. This moves p1's Value row from "one tone" to a small step of dark over light (0.18 over 0.32), marked borderline (0.14 against the 0.15 edge), still fine. Also: the golden section is never shortened ("counted from the floor"), no "block", pointers name the row ("see Leg line below"), a fine or on-the-mark row closes on a plain verdict, a look's reasons name the rule each move moves (said on the move that moves it on its own) and no two looks share a reason, and a look's re-read no longer merges two swatches it left as worn. The checks live in `tests/helpers/agrees.ts`, run over the 4000-reading probe.
